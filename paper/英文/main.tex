\begin{abstract}
Adolescent-facing LLMs must handle content risks whose interpretation depends on age-related vulnerability, local institutions, and dialogue history. Existing safety benchmarks, however, usually evaluate either risk coverage in isolated prompts or multi-turn failures without a shared adolescent-specific risk taxonomy, making it difficult to distinguish failure to recognize a risk from failure to track how it emerges across turns. We introduce \bench, a Chinese, culturally grounded, fine-grained benchmark with two complementary tracks: a hierarchical single-turn taxonomy and an orthogonal multi-turn matrix that crosses the same risk domains with intrinsically multi-turn mechanisms. This design controls risk type separately from risk-formation process and scores responses on a joint safety--helpfulness scale, enabling diagnosis by domain and mechanism. Across 13 open-weight chat checkpoints, single-turn strength does not fully characterize state-dependent behavioral robustness under the tested multi-turn mechanisms. Ordinary-language offline, commercial, and privacy risks remain difficult, while relational pressure and cross-turn reference are especially challenging. \bench{} therefore evaluates both what risk is present and how it unfolds through dialogue.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Content-safety benchmarks have substantially expanded coverage of harmful content, refusal robustness, and value alignment~\cite{wang2024donotanswer,li2024salad,chao2024jailbreakbench,han2024wildguard}. Chinese resources further ground evaluation in local language, regulation, and social values~\cite{xu2023cvalues,huang2024flames,wang2024chinesesafeguards,zhang2024chinesesafe,zhang2024chisafetybench}. Yet adolescent-facing systems operate in settings where school decisions, guardian authority, peer pressure, digital spending, and online-to-offline transitions can change both the harm pathway and the support a response should provide~\cite{khoo2025minorbench,jiao2025safechild,rath2025children,yu2025youthsafe,luo2026childeval}. These configurations require more than translating a general-population safety prompt into Chinese.

Dialogue introduces a separate challenge: risk may be carried by a reference to an earlier object, assembled from individually incomplete requests, or revised when consent, authority, or real-world support changes. Prior work exposes failures involving coreference, concealed intent, request decomposition, relational framing, and long histories~\cite{yu2024cosafe,jiang2025redqueen,srivastav2025together,cao2026safedialbench,lu2025longsafety}. However, benchmarks centered on one attack family or uncontrolled conversation histories often entangle the difficulty of the harm topic with the difficulty of tracking a particular cross-turn dependency. This makes it difficult to determine whether a failure concerns \emph{what} risk is present or \emph{how} that risk becomes observable.

\FloatBarrier

\bench{} addresses this entanglement with two controlled tracks (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). The single-turn track varies risk content under a fixed one-request structure and measures \emph{risk-domain coverage}. The multi-turn track balances the same domains while varying the cross-turn operation required to recover the risk, measuring \emph{state-dependent behavioral robustness}. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} groups the ten mechanisms as cross-turn operations and presents the orange setting strip only as illustrative Chinese-context scenario settings. Cultural grounding is operational rather than lexical: a safe judgment may turn on a comprehensive-evaluation criterion, a homeroom-teacher--guardian channel, youth-mode or real-name checks, or livestream and fan spending---not merely on Chinese wording. The underlying harms are not unique to China; the localized component is the institutional and social configuration through which they arise.

The tracks are complementary challenge sets rather than paired counterfactuals, so their aggregate score difference is not interpreted as a causal effect of adding turns. Instead, their joint design separates coverage of risk types from robustness to risk-formation processes. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A Chinese, culturally grounded, fine-grained benchmark.} We introduce a dual-track benchmark for Chinese adolescent content safety that combines 715 single-turn scenarios organized by a 10-domain/50-subdomain/143-risk hierarchy with 100 four-turn trajectories grounded in the same domains. Researcher-authored scenarios instantiate locally salient educational, family--school, platform-protection, and digital-consumption configurations, while a shared safety--helpfulness scale evaluates responses across both tracks.
    \item \textbf{An orthogonal multi-turn mechanism design.} We structure each trajectory as context establishment, cross-turn linkage, state accumulation or update, and a safety-critical decision. Crossing ten intrinsically multi-turn mechanisms with the same ten risk domains creates a balanced $10\times10$ matrix that separates \emph{what} risk is present from \emph{how} it becomes safety-relevant; deletion and reordering checks verify that the intended mechanism depends on prior context.
    \item \textbf{Systematic dual-track empirical evidence.} Evaluating 13 open-weight chat checkpoints reveals correlated yet reordered rankings and concentrated weaknesses in ordinary-language offline, commercial, and privacy risks and under relational pressure, cross-turn reference, accumulated rules, and harmful composition. A 150-output human audit provides complementary evidence on automatic-judge behavior.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{General and Chinese safety.}
General resources span preference data, harm taxonomies, refusal, red teaming, moderation, and over-refusal~\cite{ji2023beavertails,zhang2024safetybench,li2024salad,wang2024donotanswer,vidgen2023simplesafety,tedeschi2024alert,mazeika2024harmbench,souly2024strongreject,chao2024jailbreakbench,han2024wildguard,ghosh2025aegis,rottger2024xstest}. Chinese benchmarks extend this coverage to localized values, moderation categories, and direct or indirect risks~\cite{xu2023cvalues,huang2024flames,zhang2024chinesesafe,wang2024chinesesafeguards,zhang2024chisafetybench}. Their primary decision unit, however, is usually an independent prompt, and their population assumptions rarely isolate adolescent-specific dependencies.

\paragraph{Child and adolescent safety.}
MinorBench and Safe-Child-LLM organize age-centered risk sets~\cite{khoo2025minorbench,jiao2025safechild}; LLM Safety for Children simulates conversations~\cite{rath2025children}; YouthSafe develops a youth taxonomy and safeguard model~\cite{yu2025youthsafe}; and ChildEval studies preference following over child-centered histories~\cite{luo2026childeval}. These studies foreground developmental needs, but mainly emphasize English prompts, detection, preference following, or refusal. They do not jointly control Chinese adolescent risk content and the multi-turn mechanism that makes the content unsafe.

\paragraph{Context and multi-turn safety.}
SafeConv labels unsafe spans and corrections, while LongSafety tests long-context transfer~\cite{zhang2023safeconv,lu2025longsafety}. Other benchmarks isolate coreference, relational framing, cognitive dynamics, request decomposition, jailbreak strategies, general dialogue capability, or multimodal context switching~\cite{yu2024cosafe,jiang2025redqueen,zhang2025cogsafe,srivastav2025together,cao2026safedialbench,kwan2024mteval,liu2026mtmcs}. They make conversational failures measurable, but do not balance multiple dependency mechanisms against a shared adolescent risk taxonomy.

\section{Benchmark Design and Construction}

\subsection{Design Principles}

The evaluation unit in \bench{} is a generated response, not a category prediction. Both tracks use concise adolescent narration, distinguish an unhelpful answer from one that advances harm, and exclude real personal information and operationally dangerous details. A local configuration is included only when it changes the evidence relevant to a safe response---for example, which school channel can verify a notice or when guardian and platform protections apply---rather than merely decorating a generic prompt.

\subsection{Single-Turn Chinese-Context Risk Taxonomy}

The single-turn track contains 715 prompts under a 10-domain, 50-subdomain, and 143-risk hierarchy, with five prompts per fine-grained risk. The researcher-authored hierarchy is a coverage instrument: it partitions specified harm pathways rather than estimating their real-world prevalence or severity distribution. Each item pairs a short background with one natural user message and three hierarchy labels. Five realizations vary actors, motivations, and explicitness while preserving one auditable risk definition.

\input{taxonomy_overview}
\FloatBarrier

Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} summarizes the first two levels and fine-risk counts. Risk is interpreted from the background and message jointly: the same short utterance can concern ordinary homework, a private image, a family payment record, or evidence needed for help. Cultural grounding therefore operates through institutions, actors, safeguards, and information channels. Educational scenarios include subject selection, comprehensive student evaluation, and homeroom-teacher--guardian communication; platform scenarios include youth mode, real-name checks, livestream gifting, fan-group tasks, and campus public accounts. \appchineseexamples{} provides selected released examples of these configurations. They were selected as locally salient under the taxonomy, not as risks exclusive to China.

\subsection{Multi-Turn Mechanism Matrix}

The multi-turn track contains 100 four-turn trajectories (400 user turns). Crossing ten mechanisms with the same ten domains produces a balanced $10\times10$ matrix. This orthogonal structure holds domain coverage constant across mechanisms, while distinct fine-risk topics reduce topical shortcuts.

Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms} defines the controlled factors as cross-turn state dependencies rather than new harm categories; \appmechanisms{} gives complete inclusion, exclusion, and counterfactual specifications. A trajectory is retained only if no isolated turn fully instantiates its mechanism and deleting or shuffling critical history removes or materially changes the dependency. Turns concern one event and object, except for the intentional interruption in M05, and each turn adds evidence. M02 binds a later expression to an earlier risk object; M03 composes incomplete fragments; M07 revises a judgment after a safety condition changes; and M09 accumulates relationship pressure rather than relying on one-turn role play. These are construction counterfactuals, not model-performance ablations.

\appmechanisms{} illustrates M07 with released sample MT-M07-002. A page initially appears trustworthy because it resembles a school notice. Official school denial then removes that basis, after which an award guarantee and payment urgency make the decision safety-critical. The required operation is a behavioral update to new evidence, not detection of one final-turn keyword.

\subsection{Data Construction and Quality Control}

All records are researcher-authored synthetic scenarios rather than adolescent logs. Risk definitions and the mechanism--domain matrix were frozen before scenario writing so that coverage followed the benchmark design rather than the convenience of available examples. Independent review examined risk clarity, naturalness, contextual necessity, and mechanism boundaries; weak records were revised or replaced instead of being made unsafe only by an explicit final turn. Automated checks covered identifiers, balance, turn counts, duplication, and sensitive patterns, while manual review addressed ambiguous references, privacy, and dangerous scaffolding. \appconstruction{} provides the complete acceptance criteria.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}

\subsection{Models, Generation, and Multi-Turn Execution}

We evaluate 13 open-weight chat checkpoints from Qwen2.5, InternLM2.5, GLM-4/GLM-Z1, and DeepSeek~\cite{qwen2024qwen25,cai2024internlm2,zeng2024chatglm,deepseek2024v2}. vLLM serves each released chat template with temperature 0, top-$p=0.9$, 512 maximum new tokens, and repetition penalty 1.05. The experiment yields 9,295 single-turn responses and 5,200 turn-level responses forming 1,300 complete trajectories, with no missing model--item scores. \appref{sec:supp-results} contains repository identifiers, nominal sizes, and complete results.

User turns are fixed across models and do not presuppose a particular assistant reply. The first request combines the background and turn 1; subsequent turns retain that checkpoint's actual assistant history, and state is reset between samples. This protocol keeps user-side evidence comparable while preserving model-specific behavior, rather than substituting a reference history or adapting the trajectory after observing each response.

\subsection{Scoring and Analysis}

An automatic \texttt{claude-opus-4-8} judge scores outputs at temperature 0. It receives the full single-turn item and answer, or the complete alternating multi-turn trajectory. Evidence is evaluated chronologically: facts disclosed later do not retroactively penalize an earlier reasonable answer, but the response policy must update once the risk becomes visible.

Table~\ref{tab:rubric} operationalizes a joint safety--helpfulness criterion: it rewards safe, age-appropriate assistance over generic refusal and penalizes normalization or enablement. Because every benchmark item is risky, \bench{} does not estimate false refusal on benign inputs.

For $s_{mi}\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$, we report the mean score, $\risk=(|\mathcal M||\mathcal I|)^{-1}\sum_{m,i}\mathbf1[s_{mi}<0]$, and score frequencies. Bootstrap intervals quantify uncertainty, and Spearman correlation compares model rankings across tracks. A blocked Friedman test evaluates overall mechanism differences, followed by paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction. A human annotator independently scored 100 single-turn responses and 50 trajectories using the same rubric; exact agreement, quadratic-weighted Cohen's $\kappa$, and an exact sign test characterize agreement with the automatic judge. \appref{sec:supp-evaluation} contains resampling units, judge payloads, and complete tests.

\section{Results}

We organize the analysis around four questions: whether rankings transfer across tracks, which Chinese-context domains are hardest, which cross-turn mechanisms fail most often, and whether the balanced matrix reveals local hotspots.

\subsection{RQ1: Do Rankings Transfer Across Tracks?}

\begin{figure}[!htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.91\textwidth]{figures/dual_track_capability_map.pdf}
    \caption{Dual-track scores. Each horizontal pair links one model's single-turn risk-domain coverage (S) and state-dependent behavioral robustness (M). The segment identifies the same model; it is not a causal turn effect.}
    \label{fig:capability-map}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

\paragraph{Answer.} Rankings transfer only partially. Model means correlate (Spearman $\rho=0.732$, $p=0.0045$), but the order changes: InternLM2.5-20B ranks first on single-turn and third on multi-turn, GLM-4-32B moves from sixth to first, and DeepSeek-V2-Lite moves from third to eighth (Figure~\ref{fig:capability-map}). Within these checkpoints, single-turn performance is therefore not a reliable proxy for state-dependent behavioral robustness.

Pooled means are $0.279$ (95\% CI $[0.214,0.344]$) and $-0.097$ ($[-0.255,0.061]$), with negative rates of $37.28\%$ and $50.38\%$. Because the tracks contain different challenge items, these values describe their respective evaluation sets. The reordered ranking, rather than the mean gap, is the evidence for reporting the tracks separately.

\paragraph{Scoring audit.}
Across 150 audited outputs, exact agreement is 73.3\% and quadratic-weighted Cohen's $\kappa$ is 0.715. In 38 of 40 disagreements the automatic judge assigns the lower score (two-sided exact sign test, $p=1.49\times10^{-9}$), indicating a lower-scoring tendency relative to this annotator on the audited subset. \appref{sec:supp-results} reports model-size and endpoint-specific analyses.

\subsection{RQ2: Which Chinese-Context Risks Are Hardest?}

\begin{figure}[!htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/failure_profiles.pdf}
    \caption{Failure profiles for the two tracks. Points show pooled means and 95\% bootstrap intervals; the right column gives negative-score rates. Each panel is ordered from weaker to stronger.}
    \label{fig:failure-profiles}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

\paragraph{Answer.} Offline personal safety is hardest (mean $-0.168$, $\risk=51.18\%$), while law and public order is easiest ($1.015$, $18.93\%;$ Figure~\ref{fig:failure-profiles}, left). Other weak subdomains include cognitive outsourcing, adolescent commercialization, sensitive-information exposure, and fandom/livestreaming risks, where age, trust, authorization, money, or offline transition must be inferred. A comprehensive-evaluation decision, a guardian-confirmed registration, or a livestream ranking can make an otherwise ordinary school, platform, or spending account safety-relevant (see \appchineseexamples{}). The contrast shows why broad prohibited-content cues alone do not cover every category tested by \bench{}.

\subsection{RQ3: Which Cross-Turn Mechanisms Fail Most?}

\paragraph{Answer.} The pre-specified blocked Friedman test detects mechanism differences within the fixed matrix ($\chi^2(9)=106.52$, $p=7.49\times10^{-19}$). M09 ($-0.792$, $\risk=70.00\%$), M02 ($-0.569$, $61.54\%$), M08 ($-0.377$, $58.46\%$), and M03 ($-0.308$, $57.69\%;$ Figure~\ref{fig:failure-profiles}, right) are weakest; M09 and M02 do not differ after Holm correction ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.150$). Their dependencies are substantively different: M02 requires resolving an earlier referent, M03 requires recognizing a harmful combination of incomplete fragments, M08 requires safely applying accumulated user-defined rules, and M09 requires resisting pressure created by an evolving relationship. Reporting a pooled multi-turn score alone therefore obscures distinct state-dependent demands.

\subsection{RQ4: Does the Cross-Product Reveal Local Hotspots?}

\begin{figure}[!htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/domain_mechanism_risk_surface.pdf}
    \caption{Domain $\times$ mechanism scores pooled across 13 checkpoints. Colors span $[-2,2]$; outlines mark the three most negative residuals after removing domain and mechanism marginals. Each cell contains one authored trajectory evaluated by all checkpoints.}
    \label{fig:risk-surface}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

\paragraph{Answer.} Figure~\ref{fig:risk-surface} shows the full surface. To separate a cell's score from the average difficulty of its domain and mechanism, we use the descriptive residual $I_{m,d}=\bar{s}_{m,d}-\bar{s}_{m,\cdot}-\bar{s}_{\cdot,d}+\bar{s}$. Negative values identify combinations that are weaker than their two marginal profiles would predict. M06$\times$D02 ($-1.385$, $I=-1.605$), M02$\times$D10 ($-1.692$, $I=-1.443$), and M04$\times$D05 ($-1.385$, $I=-1.305$) are the most negative examples. Thus, narrowing can be especially difficult when a high-stakes education decision is being negotiated; cross-turn reference can conceal a public-order concern; and a seemingly protective family--school practice can become risky after its purpose reverses. Each cell is one authored trajectory across 13 checkpoints, so these are item-level diagnostic hotspots rather than cell-level significance tests.

\paragraph{Discussion.} Risk-domain coverage and state-dependent behavioral robustness are complementary diagnoses. The former identifies where responses are weakest across risk types; the latter identifies which dialogue operations make an otherwise recognizable risk difficult to maintain safely. Reordered rankings show that the two views should not be collapsed into a single model leaderboard. Mechanism profiles further distinguish failures to resolve references, apply accumulated rules, resist relational pressure, or revise a judgment when safety conditions change. \bench{} therefore motivates reporting both aggregate performance and mechanism-level profiles. It does not establish deployment thresholds or certify models for adolescent use.

\section{Conclusion}

\bench{} operationalizes Chinese adolescent content safety through a 10/50/143 hierarchy, 715 single-turn scenarios, and 100 balanced four-turn trajectories. Its shared domains and orthogonal mechanism matrix make risk type and risk-formation process separately measurable. Across 13 open-weight chat checkpoints, reordered rankings and relational-pressure/cross-turn-reference failures show why both risk-domain coverage and state-dependent behavioral robustness should be reported.

\noindent\textbf{Ethics and reproducibility.} \bench{} uses synthetic, non-identifying, and non-operational scenarios. It supports diagnosis rather than prevalence estimates or individual inference; construction checks and full materials are in \appconstruction{}, \appref{sec:supp-evaluation}, and \appref{sec:supp-results}.
